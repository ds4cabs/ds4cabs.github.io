About

Yongli Shan is a drug discovery scientist who takes small-molecule and biologics programs from target identification to clinical candidate nomination. He has led discovery biology, platform biology, and pharmacology across oncology, immuno-oncology, inflammation & immunology (I&I), cardiovascular disease, and cystic fibrosis.

He specializes in new modalities for hard-to-drug targets — induced proximity (PROTACs, molecular glues, DUBTACs), protein homeostasis (degradation and stabilization), and antibody- and degrader-drug conjugates (ADC, DAC). He builds AI-driven drug design (AIDD) for induced proximity: a physics-based design language for ternary and higher-order complexes and a foundation model of induced-proximity geometries, spanning GPU/TPU campaigns, free-energy and PMF methods, and linker design. He is equally at home at the bench, in the structure, and in the code.

Mentoring interests

Yongli mentors in the CABS Data Science Summer Internship Program, where he was a 2026 mentor and received the Mentor Impact Award. His mentoring interests span AI-driven drug design for induced proximity and targeted protein degradation, structure- and physics-based modeling of molecular complexes, and connecting wet-lab biology, structural biology, and computation.

He enjoys helping students turn a biological question into a model and working code, build analyses that hold up from first principles, and grow into independent scientists who move comfortably between the bench, the structure, and the code.
