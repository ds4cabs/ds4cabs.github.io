About

Shucheng (Bangli) Cao builds computational methods for one job: telling real effects from artefacts. Causal inference on data from hundreds of thousands of people, machine-learning models evaluated adversarially, and — most recently — AI agents whose every claim can be audited.

Biomedicine is where he has applied them longest, and where wrong answers cost the most: drug-target discovery, medical imaging (a dataset now used by 4,000+ teams worldwide), molecular simulation. He is a fifth-year PhD candidate in Quantitative Life Sciences at McGill, finishing in 2027.

1,000+ citations · reviewer for eight journals · CA$350K+ in competitive funding

His habit, across all of it: build it, then try to break it.

What he built at CABS

The stakes. Nearly every drug works by hitting a protein. Pick the wrong one and you find out years — and hundreds of millions — later. Genetics can tell you earlier: targets with genetic support succeed about 2.6× more often.

Two problems, not one. That evidence is scattered across dozens of databases; assembling it takes a week of manual copying per protein. AI can read it in seconds — and will invent an effect size just as fluently. Solving the labor problem alone makes the hallucination problem worse.

His answer. OpenCausal, the causal-evidence core of CausalSentinel (built with fellow intern Natalie Huang): one protein + one disease in, eight public databases queried live — the most in this year's cohort — one evidence page out, ending in go / no-go. A week becomes a minute.

The rule. The model writes two sentences. Code writes everything else. A claim with no source doesn't get flagged — the page is thrown away.

Built as a prototype. Four versions, each forced by a real failure of the last, every change judged one way: identical input, compared output. Version one — where the model invented a safety conclusion — is kept in the repo on purpose, as the before.

Checked by attack. He pointed 41 adversarial AI agents at his own system. They found 18 real defects; his own checks had caught none. All 18 fixed.

Shipped. A free web app and public site, no account needed · 991 protein dossiers indexing 101,543 published causal estimates · a first reality check: on twenty targets history has already judged, every GO was a drug that launched, and no failure was backed · all MIT-licensed.

What's next

The way he sees the work: biology defines the problem. Mathematics decides how deeply you understand it. AI agents decide how fast — and how accurately — you can solve it. And business decides whether the value is ever seen. He wants to work where those four meet: AI for science, and machine learning.

He graduates in 2027, and is always open to a conversation — about the science, the system, or growing CausalSentinel into an innovation and entrepreneurship project. Reach out via the links above.
