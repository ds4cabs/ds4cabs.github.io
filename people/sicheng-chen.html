About

Sicheng Chen’s research interests lie at the interface of computational biology, pharmacology, and data-driven drug discovery. He recently completed a Bachelor of Pharmaceutical Science at Monash University with High Distinction and will begin an MPhil in Biological Science (Pharmacology) at the University of Cambridge in October 2026.

He is particularly interested in how computational approaches can connect large-scale biological data with molecular mechanisms and support more rigorous reasoning in drug discovery.

2026 CABS Internship Project

During the 2026 CABS Data Science Summer Internship Program, Sicheng developed the LUAD Target Evidence Dossier, a provenance-aware framework for organizing heterogeneous evidence in lung adenocarcinoma.

The project was motivated by a methodological question: when evidence from multiple datasets and databases is brought together, how can we preserve where each observation came from, how it was generated, and whether apparently separate pieces of evidence are actually independent?

Starting from a governed TCGA-LUAD transcriptomic cohort, Sicheng built an evidence framework spanning 29,606 genes and integrating transcriptomic and disease-association information. The workflow preserves stable gene identities, evidence states, source provenance, missingness, dependencies, and explicit limitations throughout the analysis.

Rather than producing a target ranking, the framework was designed to keep the underlying evidence structure visible so that later biological interpretation can be traced back to the original records.

Research Approach

One of the central lessons from the project was that more records do not necessarily mean more independent evidence. Multiple observations may originate from the same dataset, publication, or experimental lineage.

This led Sicheng to become increasingly interested in computational systems that not only generate results, but also make the reasoning behind those results reproducible, interpretable, and auditable.

The internship strengthened his experience in bioinformatics, reproducible scientific programming, biological database integration, and the communication of uncertainty in computational research. More importantly, it helped shape his broader interest in computational approaches that connect evidence across different biological scales.

Looking Ahead

At Cambridge, Sicheng plans to further explore computational pharmacology, GPCR biology, structural bioinformatics, receptor conformational dynamics, and context-dependent signalling. He is particularly interested in understanding how molecular structure and cellular context influence receptor function and drug response, and how computational modelling and machine learning can help investigate these relationships.

Looking ahead, Sicheng hopes to pursue a PhD in computational pharmacology or a closely related field. His long-term goal is to contribute to drug discovery by developing computational approaches that connect molecular mechanisms with large-scale biological evidence, enabling therapeutic hypotheses to be evaluated in a more transparent, mechanistically informed, and reproducible way.
