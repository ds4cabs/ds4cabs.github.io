About

I am a Bioinformatics Ph.D. candidate at the University of California, Irvine, focusing on building disease gene networks and in-silico perturbation frameworks to accelerate therapeutic discovery. My research sits at the intersection of functional biology and machine learning, with the goal of predicting cellular responses to genetic and chemical perturbations before they reach the bench.

My thesis takes a data-driven approach to studying disease progression through gene regulatory networks. I develop computational frameworks that integrate single-cell and multi-omic data to infer regulatory architecture and identify disease-associated driver gene modules. By computationally perturbing these modules, I enable virtual experiments that allow systematic exploration of perturbation space and prioritization of therapeutic targets based on predicted system-level effects rather than isolated gene associations.

A central theme of my work is moving beyond descriptive analyses toward mechanistic and predictive models of cellular behavior. I am particularly interested in graph-based learning, network-aware machine learning, and AI-driven representations of cellular systems that preserve biological structure while scaling to high-dimensional data. These approaches provide a foundation for virtual cell models that can support hypothesis generation, experimental design, and decision-making in drug discovery.

I am especially excited by the emergence of AI agents for biology, which integrate foundation models with network reasoning and iterative hypothesis testing to guide discovery. I view in-silico perturbation and virtual cell frameworks as critical components of this ecosystem.

Through the CABS Data Science Summer Intern Program, I hope to collaborate with and learn from industry teams working at the frontier of AI-enabled drug development, while applying my research background to real-world biopharma challenges and contributing to a community committed to innovation, mentorship, and diversity.
