About

Environmental Toxicology student at the University of California, Davis, with a deep interest in drug development, pathology, and the science of how substances interact with living systems. My academic path is driven by a fascination with how environmental toxins and chemical compounds affect human health at a biological level, and a commitment to translating that curiosity into research that matters.

My interest in medicine began on the ground floor. Volunteering in the Emergency Department at Sutter Health exposed me early to the realities of frontline healthcare, watching nurses navigate high-pressure, critical situations with composure and precision. That environment sparked something, a pull toward medicine and human health that I knew I wanted to pursue seriously.

That direction was solidified in the UC Davis Comparative Pathology Lab, where I worked as a Research Intern and experienced firsthand what it means to do science with real clinical stakes. Performing necropsies and PCR experiments gave me direct exposure to pathological processes and the biological mechanisms behind disease and toxicity. Designing and executing my own research projects on environmental efficacy taught me how to move from a question to a data-backed answer. It was in that lab that I understood the space where I want to build my career, where rigorous bench science meets the goal of improving human health outcomes.

Alongside my research and pre-health pursuits, I serve as a Project Manager in the Davis Undergraduate Engineering Network (DUEN), a role I stepped into during my freshman year. Leading cross-functional teams through hands-on project development has built my ability to communicate across disciplines, manage complex timelines, and contribute meaningfully in collaborative, fast-moving environments.

I am eager to bring my laboratory background, clinical perspective, and research-driven mindset to opportunities at the intersection of toxicology, drug development, and medicine.
